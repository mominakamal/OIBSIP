Ada Lovelace

The world's first computer programmer

Biography

Augusta Ada King, Countess of Lovelace, was born in London in 1815, the only legitimate child of the poet Lord Byron. Her mother, Annabella Milbanke, encouraged Ada's education in mathematics and logic from a young age, partly to steer her away from the perceived instability of her father's poetic temperament.

In her teens, Ada became acquainted with Charles Babbage, the inventor of the Analytical Engine, an early mechanical general-purpose computer. She was fascinated by his work and began a long correspondence and collaboration with him that would define her legacy.

Between 1842 and 1843, Ada translated an article about the Analytical Engine written by Italian engineer Luigi Menabrea. She added her own extensive notes to the translation, which ended up being three times longer than the original article. Within these notes, she described an algorithm for the Analytical Engine to compute Bernoulli numbers — a sequence of rational numbers — which is now recognized as the first algorithm intended to be carried out by a machine.

Ada also wrote about the broader potential of computing machines, suggesting they could one day be used to manipulate symbols and create music or art, not just crunch numbers. This visionary idea, remarkably ahead of its time, is why she is remembered as a pioneer of computer science more than a century before the first modern computers were built.

Key Milestones

1815

Born in London, daughter of Lord Byron and Annabella Milbanke.

1833

Meets Charles Babbage and learns about his Difference Engine.

1842

Begins translating Menabrea's article on the Analytical Engine.

1843

Publishes her notes, including the first published algorithm for a machine.

1852

Passes away at age 36, later becoming a namesake of the Ada programming language.
